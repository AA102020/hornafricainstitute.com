Climate resilience and natural resources

Environmental shocks become public crises when institutions, infrastructure, and markets fail to protect vulnerable populations and essential systems

About the program

Climate variability and environmental degradation reshape livelihoods, mobility, food systems, public finance, political relations, and competition over land and water. The consequences depend on infrastructure, institutional capacity, social protection, access to information, and the distribution of political and economic resources.

The program integrates environmental science with policy research and institutional analysis. Its purpose is to strengthen climate preparedness, improve resource governance, and identify adaptation strategies suited to regional conditions.

Research areas

Climate security

Compound risks, vulnerability, conflict-sensitive adaptation, displacement pressures, and institutional preparedness.

Water governance

Surface and groundwater systems, shared resources, allocation choices, infrastructure, and cooperative governance.

Drought and food systems

Monitoring, early warning, pastoral and agricultural adaptation, land management, preparedness, and recovery.

Environmental sustainability

Watersheds, ecosystems, biodiversity, urban environmental pressure, and connections among water, energy, and food systems.

Adaptation institutions

Public capability, climate finance, local governance, program implementation, and accountability for resilience investments.

Climate data and decisions

Remote sensing, hydrological information, risk indicators, scenario analysis, and communication under uncertainty.

Research and policy analysis

The program develops climate-risk assessments, vulnerability analysis, water-resource scenarios, early-warning frameworks, adaptation strategies, and institutional recommendations. The Foresight Lab supports the work through geospatial analysis, remote sensing, hydrological modeling, forecasting, simulation, and integrated data systems.

Key questions

Building resilience before shocks become emergencies

  • Which populations, institutions, and systems face the greatest risk?
  • How can early warning produce earlier action?
  • Which institutions can govern shared water equitably?
  • How should adaptation address conflict and displacement?
  • What investments strengthen long-term resilience?
  • How can stronger evidence improve climate decisions?
