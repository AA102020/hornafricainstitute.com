Program 03

Climate Resilience, Environment, and Water

Applied research on climate security, water systems, drought preparedness, environmental sustainability, and locally grounded adaptation.

CLIMATE RESILIENCE

Environmental change is also an institutional, economic, and security challenge.

Program perspective

Climate variability affects livelihoods, mobility, food systems, public finance, political relations, and competition over land and water. Effective adaptation therefore requires integrated analysis across environmental and social systems.

Our work connects scientific and computational methods with community knowledge, policy design, and institutional capacity. The aim is to improve anticipation, resource governance, and practical resilience.

Focus areas

Climate security

Risk assessment, vulnerability mapping, conflict-sensitive adaptation, and institutional planning for compound shocks.

Water systems

Hydroinformatics, river and groundwater analysis, demand assessment, and cooperative management of shared resources.

Drought adaptation

Monitoring, early warning, adaptive agriculture, land management, preparedness, and recovery planning.

Environmental sustainability

Water-energy-food-ecosystem relationships, watershed protection, biodiversity, and long-term resource equity.

Methods and tools

The program draws on hydrological modeling, remote sensing, GIS, satellite data, vulnerability analysis, stochastic models, scenario planning, participatory research, and multi-criteria decision methods. Collaboration with Foresight Lab strengthens forecasting and integrates environmental data with social and institutional indicators.

Applied outcomes

Resilience built through evidence and cooperation

  • Climate-risk and vulnerability assessments
  • Drought monitoring and early-warning design
  • Water-allocation and resource scenarios
  • Conflict-sensitive adaptation strategies
  • Institutional climate-security frameworks
  • Community-informed resilience planning
