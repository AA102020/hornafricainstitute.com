Program 01

Conflict and Stability

Research and strategic analysis of conflict systems, security institutions, terrorism, regional security, and pathways toward durable stability.

CONFLICT AND STABILITY

Security understood in political, social, economic, and environmental context.

Program perspective

The Horn of Africa occupies a strategic position across the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, Indian Ocean, and Nile Basin. Internal political struggles interact with cross-border armed networks, global trade routes, foreign military presence, resource pressures, and competing regional projects.

Our work examines how these forces produce insecurity, how institutions respond, and which interventions can reduce violence while strengthening legitimate authority and civilian protection.

Research areas

Conflict systems

Historical drivers, institutional breakdown, border tensions, armed actors, political settlements, and conflict diffusion.

Terrorism and counterterrorism

Recruitment, organizational behavior, operational patterns, counterterrorism performance, human rights, and security capacity.

Regional security architecture

Security partnerships, foreign bases, strategic infrastructure, regional competition, and institutional arrangements for cooperation.

Maritime security

Ports, sea lanes, piracy, illicit networks, coastal economies, and governance across the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

Information environments

Propaganda, misinformation, digital platforms, public narratives, and communication strategies during conflict.

Humanitarian consequences

Displacement, host communities, aid access, civilian risk, recovery, and institutional responses to prolonged crisis.

Analytical approach

The program combines political and institutional analysis with stakeholder mapping, event data, geospatial analysis, network methods, scenario development, and computational modeling. Foresight Lab capabilities support early-warning research and systematic analysis of patterns in political violence and terrorist activity.

Applied questions

From diagnosis to institutional response

  • How do local conflicts become regional security problems?
  • Which institutions can credibly mediate or enforce agreements?
  • How should security organizations adapt to changing threats?
  • How do climate and resource pressures alter conflict risk?
  • Which indicators improve early warning?
  • How can policy protect civilians while addressing armed threats?
