Cross-program initiative

Red Sea, Gulf, and Regional Connectivity

Research on the strategic systems connecting the Horn of Africa with the Red Sea, Gulf states, Arabian Peninsula, and wider maritime economy.

RED SEA AND GULF

Security, infrastructure, investment, and diplomacy across a connected region.

Purpose

The initiative examines how Gulf engagement, maritime routes, ports, infrastructure finance, food and energy systems, migration, security partnerships, and diplomatic competition reshape political authority, economic development, and regional cooperation across the Horn of Africa.

Its work connects the Institute’s three research programs and Foresight Lab. Research considers the institutional arrangements, commercial infrastructures, security practices, and investment decisions through which regional relationships acquire practical consequences.

Research priorities

Maritime security

Bab el-Mandeb, shipping risks, naval presence, coastal security, and regional maritime governance.

Ports and corridors

Port development, logistics networks, trade routes, hinterland connectivity, and infrastructure competition.

Investment and finance

Gulf investment, development finance, sovereign capital, procurement, and infrastructure governance.

Food, water, and energy

Food-security investment, energy systems, water stress, supply resilience, and shared vulnerabilities.

Security partnerships

Defense cooperation, military access, acquisition, training, external influence, and institutional consequences.

Diplomacy and competition

Mediation, alliances, strategic competition, regional organizations, and changing diplomatic arrangements.

Policy contribution

The initiative develops strategic assessments, infrastructure and investment analysis, maritime-risk studies, regional scenarios, institutional mapping, and decision support for organizations engaging across the Horn of Africa, Red Sea, and Gulf.
